Exercise two. Warmth.

<break time="2.0s" />

The second exercise turns your quiet attention to warmth in the limbs. As the small blood vessels open, the arms and legs begin to feel warm. This is real. Your hand temperature genuinely rises, often by several degrees.

<break time="1.5s" />

The phrase is. My right arm is warm. You follow the same path as before. The dominant arm first, then the other, then both, then the legs, then all together.

<break time="1.5s" />

Heaviness is not left behind. From now on, each session runs the exercises you already know, first, and then the new one. In these early days, run heaviness in its short form. Arms and legs are heavy. Then turn to warmth.

<break time="1.5s" />

What it feels like. A flush. A tingle. A sense of the hands filling. If you want to see it for yourself, a cheap stick-on thermometer strip, on the back of the hand, will show the change.

<break time="1.5s" />

The common error here is imagining an outside source of heat. A fire, a warm bath, sunlight. This works for a moment, and blocks the real skill. The warmth should arise from your attention to the limb itself, not from a picture.

<break time="1.5s" />

One caution. Do not direct warmth to any area of fresh injury, inflammation, or infection.
