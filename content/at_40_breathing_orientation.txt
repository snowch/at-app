Exercise four. Breathing.

<break time="2.0s" />

The fourth exercise leaves the breath entirely to itself.

<break time="1.5s" />

The phrase is deliberately odd. It breathes me. Every other phrase describes a state. This one removes you, as the one in charge. The moment you take command of the breath, the exercise has failed. If the phrasing does not sit well, two others keep the same passive quality. My breathing is calm and regular. Or simply, it is breathing.

<break time="1.5s" />

This is the exercise where the urge to control is strongest, because the breath is the one automatic function you can seize. You will catch yourself pacing it, deepening it, smoothing it out. Each time, notice, drop it, and return to the phrase.

<break time="1.5s" />

What it feels like. The breath becoming something watched, rather than performed. Often it slows, and grows shallower, on its own. Pauses may lengthen. Leave it all alone.

<break time="1.5s" />

By now the shoulders are released before you reach this, which is why the breathing exercise arrives when it does. It works best from a body that has already let go of the muscles that interfere.
