The neck and shoulders.

<break time="2.0s" />

This is a short addition, introduced alongside the heartbeat, and it behaves differently from the others. Understanding the difference matters.

<break time="1.5s" />

The phrase is. My neck and shoulders are heavy. Three repetitions are enough. Fewer than the limb exercises.

<break time="1.5s" />

It does not take a fixed rung on the ladder. Instead, it moves to the very end of the formula work, and stays there. From now on, every new exercise you learn is placed just ahead of it. It always comes last, just before the closing phrase.

<break time="1.5s" />

Why here? Two reasons. The shoulders drive the shallow, upper breathing that gets in the way of the breathing exercise, which comes next. Releasing them first clears the ground. And it is a gentle place to settle, after the heartbeat, which is the exercise most likely to raise unease.

<break time="1.5s" />

Why keep it at the end? Because for most modern bodies, from desks, and screens, and phones, the shoulders are where the tension gathers. So they earn the last place, where your attention is deepest, and the release is most complete.

<break time="1.5s" />

What it feels like. The shoulders dropping, often further than you expect. A widening across the collarbones. The neck lengthening, without being moved.

<break time="1.5s" />

As everywhere, nothing is done. You do not roll or adjust the shoulders. The drop happens, or it does not.
