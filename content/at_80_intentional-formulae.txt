After the six. Intentional formulae.

<break time="2.0s" />

The standard six are the foundation, not the whole building. Once they are reliable, the same passive attention can carry a phrase of your own.

<break time="1.5s" />

An intentional formula is a short, positive, present-tense phrase, introduced into the deeply relaxed state, at the end of a full session. There, it lands differently than it does in ordinary thought.

<break time="1.5s" />

Where does it go? Either just after the neck and shoulders, or after, I am at peace. Both are fine. The first places it inside the practice. The second places it at the threshold, on the way out. Try both, and keep whichever lands.

<break time="1.5s" />

A few rules make them work. Short enough to hold in one breath. Present tense, as though already true. Positively framed, with no negatives. The mind handles, I am calm around him, far better than, I do not get angry. Non-coercive. I can, and I am, rather than, I must, and I will. A little rhythm helps. And one at a time, for weeks at a stretch.

<break time="1.5s" />

Take your time to find one that speaks to something real in you. And then let the deep, quiet end of a session be the place where you plant it.
